# NEAR

Working story. Current shape is the 10–11 Oct 2025 chat. The July 2024 `NEAR:TBC` pass is kept at the bottom as earlier scale.

## Premise

A short story (a book was the earlier want) set in a late-1990s / early-2000s America after MAGA. The period is the period of the world and the tapes. Kevin's comparison: Stalinism after Leninism.

The regime is a pastiche and parody of the New Apostolic Reformation. They are fully in control of the government, at *The Handmaid's Tale* scale: church and state are the same machine. N.E.A.R. is that machine with the serial numbers filed off.

What got them there is a pastiche of Project 2025: **Plan 1985**. It does what Project 2025 actually does — muted policy language, personnel, agencies, a years-long install — not a purge brochure. Behind it, a Heritage Foundation that was as aggressive in the 1980s as the real one was in the 2020s.

The Trump-like figure is **Reagan**, gone off the rails: the face of the capture. Morning-in-America warmth is the bait. That is weaponized nostalgia aimed at the period itself.

**How history diverges (working):**

1. Real terms hold through Reagan's two (1981–1989).
2. **Plan 1985** drops in the second term as the quiet blueprint. Same register as Project 2025: reformers, mandates, staffing, restructuring. It takes years to implement. People who aren't watching think it is boring paperwork.
3. After 1988, Bush Sr. is president and Dan Quayle is VP. The Trump-era contingency pastiche: Reagan is made **Speaker of the House**, Bush and Quayle step down, Reagan returns through succession. Third term without a third election. The 22nd Amendment bars being *elected* more than twice; succession is the loophole. In-world it sounds legal, blessed, inevitable.
4. **That** is when the purges and the hard turn kick in — not when the Plan is published. The succession unlocks what the muted document made possible. Old news and anniversary tapes at story time remember *this* window, not 1985 as blood year.
5. A fourth term, if any, is not locked.

By story time (late 90s / early 2000s) the violence is history. The revolution is already over. There is nothing left to stop. The horror is the peace that came after.

Oct 2025 chat language still said MAGA / Trump for the believer's politics. In this alt-history those map onto the Reagan-N.E.A.R. machine. Diegetic branding (what people on the tapes actually say) can stay period; it does not have to say Trump.

It opens as a utopia. The point of view is a hardcore believer who is content, the way people on the pills are content in *We Happy Few*. The reader feels the dread. He does not, until his wife dies. *American Psycho* is the model for that split: the world arrives through his rose-colored account, and the omitted detail shows up after the death.

The horror is for Christians. The regime talks like Christianity and practices the opposite. The twist is a hidden "book of wisdom," secret teachings of Jesus. They are ordinary Gospel lines the regime cut out: love your enemy, be good to the immigrant. The utopia looks good because it runs on an edited scripture.

## How it opens

Third person, for now. Second person, and leaving him unnamed so he stays general, stayed in the back pocket.

He walks to church with his family on a clear morning. Crystal-blue sky, sun at the 9 a.m. angle, his wife's hand in his. He thinks about lunch after church and dinner later. He stopped keeping up with music when things were still good. The reference point he reached for was mid-2000s Christian rock, Jars of Clay and that lane.

Down the street a diner is empty and a TV is still on. Whoever was on shift left it on because everyone had to leave for service, and they will not be gone long. Attendance is required. The story never says "by law." Call it service, so a general reader can miss it and a Christian reader cannot.

He is good at this world. Following the rules costs him nothing, because the rules are already what he would do. He has no picture of someone whose day is different.

## Service

Megachurch grammar. A rock band before the sermon. The pastor talks the way a pastor talks now: conversational, soft at first, then hotter. Literary scripture-voice is the wrong sound.

The sermon is about a peace they have already won. Satan is beaten. What remains gets described as leftover evil: liberals, possessed, murdering babies. Dissent is still in the news, at the tail end of being stamped out, and the news paints protests as satanic insurrection. Politics and religion are the same sentence.

The sermon should sound extreme to people who are already extreme, by stating values. It starts on ground a listener could share with Christianity, then leans harder into the values that are Christian nationalism and sin in Jesus's teaching. By the end it is the false-Christian definition, spoken as a victory.

Opener: the pledge. Closer: a rewritten Lord's Prayer, aimed at the leaders. Kevin called that closer the ultimate horror, blasphemy said casually.

Public shame is part of the service. The pastor reads a short list of people who missed church, from a place of quoted love, and asks the room to pressure them back. It is truancy in tone. It is also how the neighbor-reporting tool pays off.

## Words he wanted kept

Pledge, committed in the 11 Oct 2025 chat:

> I pledge allegiance to Jesus and the N.E.A.R. And to his sacrifice through which I'm saved. One nation sanctified, single-minded, with liberty for the faithful and triumph over all.

He wanted the words simple, because the people who feel this way have to be able to say them. "Triumph" won over "dominion" and "conquest" because it sounded softer and sanctified. The flag of the N.E.A.R. is a different flag. He was thinking of the pine-tree flag.

The Lord's Prayer was still moving. Last lean, not locked:

> Our founders, who were granted divine leadership, hallowed be your guidance. Your vision has come. Your will was done in the lands of the nation you rule. Grant us today our daily duty. As we stand steadfast against evil, forgive only those who are deserving. Guide us from deception. Deliver us from unity. For your power is the will of the everlasting God, forever and ever.

## Reporting a neighbor

Snitch and shame stay. The tool is a tip line: an 800 or 900 number. You call to request mercy for yourself, or to help a neighbor back into God's love. Forgiveness stays out of the copy, because forgiveness is one of the things this Bible removed. The pastor gets the name.

On the walk to church the protagonist reports someone. At the end of service that name is called. He stands and gives a small wave.

The Oct 2025 chat had a pocket app and a photo. That form is out. The function is the same.

## The turn

His wife is afraid. He is sure they will be the exception. She is not, and she is furious with him for it.

She has an ectopic pregnancy. The refusal is callous for a policy reason he liked: the procedure would take away her ability to have children, and they are not authorized to do that to a woman. She dies. Their children are taken, because a father is not allowed to raise children without a mother.

Her death is the crossing into the special world. She has quoted a Bible verse he has never heard, and that verse is what puts him on the search.

Afterward he has grief and no words for it. The pamphlet is about the baby ("So You Lost Your Baby"), and there is no literature for losing a partner. The fellowship wraps the bureaucracy in blessing. If he does not find a new partner in time, one will be assigned. That pressure is the incel note he asked for, biblically wrapped, bless-your-heart positive.

The shame machine catches him when the depression keeps him in bed. Someone else reports him. The rule-break starts there, before he means to break anything.

The search ends at the hidden book. The deeper truths are the cut verses.
