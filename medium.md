# Medium

NEAR is the first project in this medium. The hertus Diamond Age, the 40k-tone far future, comes after this one works.

## What the medium is

AI slop, chosen the way hertus already chose it: a real medium, the way Gossip Goblin uses it, and the lowest bar to finish and release while slower mediums wait.

The story is told from inside the bad society, in pieces that each work alone. The references for that shape are the ones already named for this kind of telling: *Buffet Infinity* (a story told through cheap local TV), *C.S.A.: The Confederate States of America* (commercials from a bigoted country), and Gossip Goblin (short-form absurd slop). The NEAR-specific look is ordinary 90s civic and church media — not analog horror.

## The look and the year

The world is late 1990s / early 2000s. Daily life and the regime's media sit in the same period. The Oct 2025 note that wanted a modern internet and an AI in your pocket is superseded.

The horror is weaponized nostalgia. Warm, familiar imagery from that window, turned into the fellowship's face. Millennials, Gen X, and Boomers should feel it in the gut. Gen Z should get a vague recognition of something half-remembered. Generation labels are only how the period is aimed. They never appear in the story, the dialogue, or the tapes.

**Not** fuzzy VHS tracking, found-footage decay, or analog-horror grain. That is the generation of look *before* this one. NEAR is what comes after: clean enough 90s **CRT** resolution, **WordArt** PowerPoints, school textbooks, local news packages, late-night infomercials, fellowship orientation decks. A pastor who smiles while he reads the list on a projected slide. A diner TV on the local affiliate. The comfort is the trap because it looks normal.

Hertus has a tabled note about a 90s corporate-training-video look as garbled relics of our era inside the far future. That note stays tabled there. This repo practices the ordinary 90s media look on a different story, before anyone tries a related texture on the 40k-tone future.

## What a piece is

One world rule, one artifact, watchable on its own. The story shows up at the edges across pieces, the way the hertus far-future ARG was meant to.

First queue, in story order. Each one is a scene he already described:

1. **The diner TV.** Empty room, local news or a station ID left on, everyone gone to service. The law is visible because nobody is there.
2. **The pledge bumper.** The locked pledge, pine-tree flag, maybe a WordArt title card; rock band or soft bumper under it.
3. **The sermon.** Conversational, then hotter. Victory over Satan. The values slide.
4. **The list.** Short. Said with love. One name Alex reported. He waves.
5. **The closer.** The rewritten prayer to the founders.
6. **So You Lost Your Baby.** The pamphlet, textbook-adjacent grief handout, or orientation PowerPoint they give the widower. No language for a dead wife. A deadline for a new one.
7. **Help a neighbor back.** The tip line, as a local-news CTA or late-night infomercial. An 800 or 900 number. Call to request mercy, or to bring a neighbor back into God's love. No forgiveness in the copy.
8. **The verse.** The line she quoted that he cannot find in his Bible. End of the queue, start of the twist. The hidden book itself waits until the world around it is solid.

Prose scenes (the walk, the marriage, the bed) stay in `world.md` until a piece needs them. They are the story between the tapes.
