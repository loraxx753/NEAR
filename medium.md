# Medium

NEAR is the first project in this medium. The hertus Diamond Age, the 40k-tone far future, comes after this one works.

## What the medium is

AI slop, chosen the way hertus already chose it: a real medium, the way Gossip Goblin uses it, and the lowest bar to finish and release while slower mediums wait.

Told from inside the bad society, in pieces that each work alone. No main character for this project yet — artifacts and broadcasts, not a protagonist arc. The references for that shape: *Buffet Infinity* (cheap local TV), *C.S.A.: The Confederate States of America* (commercials from a bigoted country), Gossip Goblin (short-form absurd slop). The NEAR-specific look is ordinary 90s civic and church media — not analog horror.

The Alex Valentine short story (walk, wife, hidden book) is a **different story in the same world**. Its prose and character pieces stay parked in `world.md`; do not fold them into the first-wave shorts.

## The look and the year

The world is late 1990s / early 2000s. Daily life and the regime's media sit in the same period. The Oct 2025 note that wanted a modern internet and an AI in your pocket is superseded.

The horror is weaponized nostalgia. Warm, familiar imagery from that window, turned into the fellowship's face. Millennials, Gen X, and Boomers should feel it in the gut. Gen Z should get a vague recognition of something half-remembered. Generation labels are only how the period is aimed. They never appear in the content, the dialogue, or the tapes.

**Not** fuzzy VHS tracking, found-footage decay, or analog-horror grain. That is the generation of look *before* this one. NEAR is what comes after: clean enough 90s **CRT** resolution, **WordArt** PowerPoints, school textbooks, local news packages, late-night infomercials, fellowship orientation decks. A pastor who smiles while he reads the list on a projected slide. A diner TV on the local affiliate. The comfort is the trap because it looks normal.

Hertus has a tabled note about a 90s corporate-training-video look as garbled relics of our era inside the far future. That note stays tabled there. This repo practices the ordinary 90s media look on a different story, before anyone tries a related texture on the 40k-tone future.

## How history shows up (and how it doesn't)

**No prestige-drama prologue.** The coup, assassination, and House storm are not a cold open that explains the world.

**First shorts (YT Shorts / Reels lane)** introduce **mundanity**: diner TV, pledge bumper, tip-line CTA, WordArt slides, empty streets at service time. Horror as normal life. The viewer should feel the period before they get the civics lesson.

**Backstory texture** — when it appears — is **History Channel / 60 Minutes** level: documentary snippets, anniversary specials, talking-head overviews, soft reenactment energy. Over-explained in the way cable news and basic-cable history are over-explained. Pamphlets and diegetic memory can still misremember; the special can lie with a calm voice.

Those doc snippets sit **later** in the release stack (or as ambient TV inside a mundane piece), not as episode one.

## What a piece is

One world rule, one artifact, watchable on its own. The world shows up at the edges across pieces, the way the hertus far-future ARG was meant to.

**First wave — mundanity.** Shorts / reels into the settled world. No protagonist required:

1. **The diner TV.** Empty room, local news or a station ID left on, everyone gone to service. The law is visible because nobody is there.
2. **The pledge bumper.** The locked pledge, pine-tree flag, maybe a WordArt title card; rock band or soft bumper under it.
3. **The sermon.** Conversational, then hotter. Victory over Satan. The values slide.
4. **The list.** Short. Said with love. Names of people who missed service; the room is asked to bring them back.
5. **The closer.** The rewritten prayer to the founders.
6. **Help a neighbor back.** The **800** tip line, as a local-news CTA or public-service bumper. Toll-free. Call to request mercy, or to bring a neighbor back into God's love. No forgiveness in the copy. **Style match (locked):** 700 Club spiritual-warfare promos + prayer-line closes + local-cable 800 call-now stacks — see shelf. Soft urgency, cosmic stakes in a calm voice, "a friend" on the other end, number repeated on screen. Same warm register as a prayer CTA; the ask is snitch-and-shame.
7. **Pray with us.** The **900** prayer line, as a late-night infomercial: WordArt, testimonials, per-minute rates, call now. Prayer requests you pay for. Same era grammar as psychic hotlines and miracle products.

**Later wave — cable history.** History Channel / 60 Minutes–grade snippets on the succession scar, the martyr, the scapegoat, Dole, the House storm. Same CRT comfort; different show bible. They deepen the world after mundanity has stuck; they do not open the project.

**Not this project (Alex Valentine story):** "So You Lost Your Baby," the verse she quotes, the wave after reporting someone on the walk, the hidden-book turn. Same world when that story runs.
